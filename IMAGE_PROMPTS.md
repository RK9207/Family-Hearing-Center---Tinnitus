## Global rules for every image on this page

These come from DESIGN_SYSTEM.md, PAGE_STRATEGY.md, CONTENT_DIRECTION.md and RESEARCH.md. They apply to every option below.

**Identity and honesty**
- No AI image may depict, resemble, or be captioned as a named clinician (Dr. Cristina Reddy, Dr. Rachel Frederick, Dr. Allison Liberio, Dr. Sara Ennis, Scott O'Leary) or be presented as "our team" or "our Flower Mound office."
- AI is not used for testimonials or clinic representation, and is not presented as a real clinical demonstration (DESIGN_SYSTEM.md, Imagery and source rules). Any AI hero image is an illustrative fallback that needs client approval.
- Do not use the homepage team photo `ChatGPT-Image-Jun-2-2026-11_25_25-PM.jpg` as an authentic team photo (RESEARCH.md, Critical Finding #5).
- No image may imply an outcome: no "relief" moment, no hands-off-ears celebration, no before-and-after.

**Visual language**
- Warm, uncluttered, real-feeling healthcare photography. It should look like a calm private practice, not a hospital.
- The color mood should sit comfortably beside the page palette: warm neutrals (#FAF6F0 and #F2EADF surfaces, #4E3110 brown headings) with soft blue accents near #197FC4, such as a sweater, a cushion, or a wall tone. Avoid saturated teal "medical" grading.
- Natural window light or soft practical light. Gentle, believable skin tones. Moderate depth of field.

**Hard exclusions (add to every negative prompt)**
Visible text, signage, logos, brand names on devices, watermarks, hearing-aid brand markings, lab coats with stethoscopes, hospital beds, IV poles, surgical lighting, exaggerated grimacing or hands clutching the head, exaggerated smiling at camera, glossy plastic skin, extra fingers, distorted ears, warped earbuds or headphones, mirrored or duplicated objects, uncanny eyes, overly symmetrical faces.

**Standard negative prompt (paste where the tool supports it)**
`text, letters, logo, watermark, signage, brand name, lab coat, stethoscope, hospital, operating room, harsh fluorescent light, teal color grade, exaggerated emotion, holding head in pain, grimace, staged smile at camera, glossy skin, plastic skin, extra fingers, deformed hands, distorted ears, malformed headphones, duplicated objects, uncanny eyes, oversharpened, HDR, stock photo look`

## IMG-HERO-01 — Hero

### Placeholder purpose
This is the strongest image-led moment on the page. It sits beside the H1 ("Tinnitus care in Flower Mound that starts with a clear look at your hearing") and must:
- Make the service feel human and approachable. The visitor should see that care starts with a conversation and an evaluation, not a sales pitch.
- Signal who and where the care comes from. A real clinician or office is ideal for this (CONTENT_DIRECTION.md, Trust cue).
- Reinforce the evaluation-first promise of understanding your results and next steps without implying a clinical outcome.
- Stay quiet enough that the headline, Book Appointment button and Google badge keep visual priority.

### Recommended source type
**Real provider/team image preferred.** AI-generated lifestyle/editorial image appropriate only as an approved illustrative fallback.

### Recommended composition
- **Orientation and aspect ratio:** 4:3 landscape on desktop. The CSS crops to 5:4 below 600 px, so supply at least 1800 × 1350 px.
- **Subject placement:** Put the main faces in the center to upper-center band, roughly 30–55% from the top. On desktop the image sits to the right of the copy, so a subject angled slightly toward the left (toward the headline) reads naturally.
- **Negative space:** Moderate. No text overlays the image, but leave calm background on at least one side so it doesn't compete with the busy content column.
- **Crop considerations:** The container has about 20 px rounded corners, so keep hands, faces and key objects away from the corners. The 5:4 mobile crop trims mostly the sides, so keep faces within the central 70% of the width.
- **Desktop/mobile:** On mobile the image appears after the headline, CTAs, badge and fact list, so it is a supporting image there, not the first impression. It still needs to read at about 350 px wide: two people at most, faces large enough to see expression.

### Option 1 — Patient-provider conversation (evaluation-first)
Photorealistic editorial healthcare photograph, 4:3 landscape. A warm, unhurried conversation in a small private audiology consultation room. An adult patient in their late 50s to mid 60s sits in a comfortable upholstered chair, angled three-quarters toward the camera, listening attentively with a calm, slightly thoughtful expression. Their posture is relaxed but engaged: leaning in a little, hands resting loosely in their lap. Across from them, partially turned away from the camera with the face seen in soft profile, an audiologist in their 30s to 40s in business-casual clothing (soft knit sweater or blouse in muted blue, no lab coat, no stethoscope) gestures lightly with an open hand as if explaining something. A small round side table between them holds a closed laptop and a glass of water; nothing clinical is in focus. Background: warm off-white walls, a light oak shelf with a plant, soft daylight from a window on the left. Shot at eye level with a 50mm lens feel, f/2.8, background softly out of focus. Natural color with a warm neutral palette and a single soft blue accent. Mood: reassuring, attentive, respectful. Realistic skin texture, no retouched perfection. Faces in the upper-center band, nothing important within 10% of the corners. No text, logos, signage or brand markings anywhere.

### Option 2 — Quiet-moment recognition (lifestyle, symptom-aware)
Photorealistic lifestyle editorial photograph, 4:3 landscape. Early evening in a tidy, warmly lit suburban North Texas living room. A man or woman in their early 50s sits at the end of a sofa with a book resting closed on their knee, gaze drifting slightly off to the side, as if noticing something in the quiet. The expression is reflective and a little distracted, not distressed: no hands on ears, no wincing. A table lamp provides warm practical light, with fading blue-hour daylight from a window behind them for gentle contrast. Wardrobe: relaxed, everyday clothing such as a soft heather-grey or muted blue sweater. The room has natural lived-in detail: a throw blanket, a mug, a couple of framed photos with no visible images or text. Shot at slight seated eye level with a 35mm lens feel, f/2.8, subject placed center-right, room softly falling off in depth. Calm, understated, documentary realism, not staged. Warm neutral palette with a quiet blue accent. No text, logos, screens with content, or brand markings.

### Option 3 — Results discussion, patient-focused (premium editorial)
Photorealistic premium healthcare editorial photograph, 4:3 landscape. A close, intimate moment of an audiologist reviewing hearing evaluation results with a patient. The camera is behind and slightly beside the audiologist, whose shoulder, upper arm and hand are in soft focus in the left third of the frame. The hand rests near a tablet held at a natural angle, and the tablet screen is blank or abstract light, unreadable. The patient, a woman or man in their early 60s, is in sharp focus at center-right, facing the audiologist and the camera at a gentle three-quarter angle. Their expression is attentive and quietly reassured, with the beginnings of understanding: a slight nod, relaxed brow, no broad smile. Wardrobe: patient in a casual collared shirt or cardigan in warm tones; audiologist in a muted blue knit. Setting: a calm consultation room with warm white walls, soft daylight from the right, a hint of light wood furniture. Shot with an 85mm lens feel, f/2.2, shallow depth of field, creamy background. Refined, warm, natural color with soft contrast. Realistic skin, natural hands, no medical props beyond the tablet. No text, numbers, charts, logos or brand markings visible.

### Recommended option
**If AI is approved as a fallback: Option 3.** It shows the page's central promise, that your audiologist goes over your results with you at the same visit. The audiologist is shown only from behind and out of focus, so the image does not stand in for any named clinician, and it creates no "this is our team" misrepresentation. The patient's quiet understanding supports the "clearer next step" message without implying a clinical result.

Option 1 matches the strategy almost as well. However, it shows a visible clinician face, which a visitor could easily read as a real Family Hearing Practice provider. Use it only with a real photo.

Option 2 is the safest for identity. However, it moves the hero toward the symptom (the job of the next section) and away from the evaluation-first message, so it fits better as a reserve image if a lifestyle visual is ever needed in the symptom section.

## IMG-PROVIDER-01 — Care options and clinicians (Dr. Cristina Reddy, AuD)

### Placeholder purpose
This image sits beside the credential "Certified to administer Tinnitus Retraining Therapy." Its only job is to put a real, recognizable face to the clinician whose certification anchors the TRT content. It makes the expertise approachable and lets visitors recognize her if they book with her. It must be her. Any generated or stand-in face would misrepresent provider identity.

The Step 4A draft already loads her real headshot from the client site (`/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Cristiana-Headshot-1.jpg`). The prompts below are **image-editing prompts applied to that real photograph**. They prepare it for the layout without changing who she is.

### Recommended source type
**Real provider/team image preferred.** Required, in fact. AI is limited to non-identity edits: crop extension, background and light harmonization.

### Recommended composition
- **Orientation and aspect ratio:** 4:5 portrait. Rendered at about 120 × 150 px on mobile and desktop, and at about half the column width between 600 and 999 px. Supply at least 800 × 1000 px.
- **Subject placement:** Head and shoulders. Eyes at roughly 35–40% from the top. The CSS uses `object-position: 50% 20%`, so keep the face in the upper-middle.
- **Negative space:** Minimal. A small margin above the head, with shoulders reaching the bottom edge.
- **Crop considerations:** The container has about 12 px rounded corners. The credentials sit in live text beside the photo, so nothing in the image should carry text.
- **Desktop/mobile:** The photo is small. Expression and face size matter more than background detail, so a tight, well-lit crop beats a wide environmental portrait.
- **Pairing:** It must sit comfortably next to IMG-PROVIDER-02 at the same crop, with similar background tone and similar lighting direction.

### Option 1 — Faithful 4:5 crop extension (minimal intervention)
Image edit of the supplied real headshot photograph. Preserve the person's face, facial features, skin tone, hair, expression, clothing and identity exactly. Do not alter, beautify, slim, smooth or regenerate any part of the person. Only extend the canvas to a 4:5 portrait aspect ratio by outpainting the existing background seamlessly: same color, texture, gradient and lighting falloff as the original backdrop. Add enough headroom that the eyes sit about 37% from the top, and extend at the bottom so the shoulders reach the frame edge naturally. Match grain and noise to the original. Output at least 800 × 1000 px. No added objects, text, logos, borders or vignettes.

### Option 2 — Warm-neutral background harmonization (pair-matching)
Image edit of the supplied real headshot photograph. Keep the person completely unchanged: face, features, skin tone, hair, expression, glasses or jewelry if present, and clothing, pixel-faithful to the source. Replace only the background with a soft, evenly lit warm-neutral studio backdrop in a light warm beige near #F2EADF, with a very gentle gradient that is slightly lighter behind the head. Refine the edge mask carefully around the hair so fine strands are preserved, with no halo or cutout edge. Adjust only global white balance so the portrait sits naturally on the warm backdrop; do not change skin tone. Crop to 4:5 portrait, head and shoulders, eyes about 37% from the top. Output at least 800 × 1000 px. No text, logos, props or added light effects.

### Option 3 — Consistent light and color grade (series match)
Image edit of the supplied real headshot photograph, to match a second clinician headshot in the same series. Do not change the person in any way: identity, facial structure, expression, skin texture and clothing stay exactly as photographed, with no retouching, smoothing or reshaping. Apply only global photographic corrections: neutral-warm white balance, gentle exposure lift on the face if underexposed, soft contrast, and natural, not punchy, saturation, so the image reads as soft window light from the front left. Keep the existing background but tone it toward a calm light neutral so it doesn't compete with the face. Crop to 4:5 portrait, head and shoulders, eyes about 37% from the top. Output at least 800 × 1000 px. No text, logos, borders or filters.

### Recommended option
**Option 1, combined with Option 3 only if needed.** Check the native aspect ratio of the real headshot first. If it already crops cleanly to 4:5 with her face upper-center, use it unedited.

If it doesn't, Option 1 is the lightest-touch fix and carries the least risk of altering her appearance. Add Option 3's global light and color pass only if she looks noticeably different in tone from Dr. Frederick's photo once the two are placed side by side. Option 2 (background replacement) is the most visible edit and has the highest risk of cutout artifacts around the hair. Reserve it for the case where the two original backgrounds clash badly.

This image identifies a named, credentialed clinician next to her certification claim. Any AI-generated person would misrepresent provider identity and undermine the trust the section exists to build.

Before launch:
